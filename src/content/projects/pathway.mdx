## Why I built it

I kept seeing avatar-based AI agents marketed with expensive licenses, and I wanted to try building one myself. Pathway is my experiment with the chatbot underneath that experience.

It uses retrieval-augmented generation, or RAG: find relevant passages in a selected knowledge base, give them to the language model, and show the sources with the answer. I used three fictional pharmaceutical-support cases to explore how useful that could be for office staff, field reimbursement managers, and patient-support teams.

I try to learn a different tool with each project. For Pathway, I chose Render to host the frontend and application server, and Supabase for PostgreSQL and pgvector. Deploying the app gave me hands-on practice connecting a static site, an API, and a persistent database.

## The experience

Choose a support role and its Knowledge Pack, then ask a question or prepare a draft. The three packs contain 42 synthetic documents covering missing paperwork, coverage and access, and specialty-pharmacy fulfillment. Each agent has a stock portrait to make its role recognizable. The photographed people are not staff or endorsers.

The recorded case snapshot stays beside the conversation. A person can ask what is missing, follow up on why it matters, or prepare an office email without losing context. On mobile, the case details open in a drawer.

Sources opens the exact supporting passages. Grounding shows what was retrieved. Live activity explains what the application is doing while an answer is prepared. Drafts can be copied or refined, and remain unsent. Earlier answers keep their original case and sources when the user changes knowledge.

## Decisions and design lessons

**The knowledge base needs useful detail.** Checklists, troubleshooting steps, and communication examples give retrieval more to work with than short topic summaries. Those guides still need to stay separate from the facts recorded for a particular case.

**A valid citation does not prove the whole answer.** Checking that a cited passage was retrieved catches one failure mode. Whether it supports the wording of every claim needs separate review.

**Hosting choices shape the experience.** A static frontend can appear before the application server is ready. Database availability also matters, even when the site itself loads. Using Render and Supabase made those dependencies concrete.

## Current state and next step

The public demo supports questions, follow-ups, source inspection, and drafts across all three scenarios. It uses fictional cases and documents, does not send messages, and does not provide clinical advice or make payer decisions.

The repository includes automated checks and a synthetic conversation evaluation, with partial results and limitations. My next evaluation focus is answers that cite the right source but overstate what it establishes.
