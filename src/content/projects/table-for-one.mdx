## The product question

A phone order looks simple until the restaurant puts someone on hold, asks about a substitution, or needs a decision that was not in the original request. Delegating the call means deciding which choices an assistant may make and when it should return control.

Table for One explores that boundary through a private takeout-calling assistant. My role combines product design and prototyping. The project is deliberately bounded around one person’s reviewed order, rather than a general agent that can purchase anything it is asked to find.

## The experience

The current experience runs in a local simulator. A person describes an order, reviews its details and spending limit, then starts a simulated call. The app shows the order’s state and supports human intervention when the conversation moves beyond the approved request. Restaurant calling remains disabled pending controlled validation.

The local simulator exercises holds, intervention, startup failures, and payment interruptions without calling a restaurant. Human takeover is designed to preserve the restaurant conversation while disconnecting the AI and privately briefing the owner. Order history records what is known; an uncertain call result must remain uncertain until there is evidence to reconcile it.

The website and durable state remain available when the voice worker is off. Call creation has its own recorded identity and outcome, so a lost response does not justify blindly dialing again. Model tools request bounded actions; they cannot silently replace the approved destination or spending limit.

## Decisions and design lessons

**Uncertainty is a real state.** A timed-out request does not prove that a call failed to start. Durable action records and evidence-based recovery matter more than making every error immediately retryable.

**Taking over is part of the core journey.** A useful assistant needs a practical way to hand the conversation back. Preserving the existing call is different from telling the user to start again.

**Payment cannot inherit conversational trust.** A fluent model response is not authorization or proof of payment. The payment laboratory uses dummy credentials and isolated test behavior; real-card automation remains a separate unresolved capability.

## Current state and next step

AWS components and the newer voice implementation are deployed, while restaurant calling remains disabled. Documented local checks cover deterministic scenarios, recovery, and transport behavior against fixtures. Those checks do not prove real restaurant interactions, payment handling, or physical-device reliability.

The next milestone is controlled voice validation with a consenting test recipient, before widening the scope. Autonomous restaurant ordering has not yet been validated.
