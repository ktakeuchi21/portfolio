## The product question

A meal can leave a photograph behind while losing the details that would make it worth revisiting: what went into it, how it tasted, and what to change next time. Recording everything in a form adds effort at the moment someone wants to eat.

What I Made explores a lighter approach to keeping a cooking history. My role is product design and hands-on prototyping. It is built for home cooks who want to remember individual attempts and discover patterns across the dishes they make.

## The experience

Capture starts with a photograph and dish name. A cook can add a natural voice note, review the suggested fields, and save without accepting every suggestion. One occasion can contain several dishes and photographs.

The same archive supports three perspectives: a chronological Journal, a photographic Year view, and a culinary Map. Dish histories connect repeat attempts; filters help rediscover a meal. Recipe Ideas can be imported or researched when requested, then saved as editable snapshots. Backup and restore give the owner a way to retain the archive outside the browser.

## Decisions and design lessons

**Capture effort is a product constraint.** Photo and name form a minimal path. More capable assistance is useful only if it reduces effort without making the cook correct a long, unexpected form.

**Suggestions should remain suggestions.** The person can inspect and edit AI-proposed details. Deterministic local matching and country resolution handle tasks that do not need a model’s judgment.

**Local ownership comes with responsibilities.** Device-local storage protects the archive from routine cloud upload, but sign-in alone does not provide cross-device backup. Export and validated restore are part of the product, not an afterthought.

## Current state and next step

The invitation-only PWA has a publicly accessible fictional sample. Repository records describe owner iPhone validation and client/service checks, with remaining device checks documented separately. A public sample demonstrates the experience without exposing a private cooking history.

A useful next question is whether repeat cooks can recover a specific past attempt quickly, especially as dish names and occasions accumulate. The [public repository](https://github.com/ktakeuchi21/what-i-made) includes the product requirements, architecture, and media attribution.
