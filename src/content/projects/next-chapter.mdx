## Why I built it

My wife’s aunt runs a senior downsizing business. Helping someone move also means finding new homes for their belongings: furniture, appliances, collectibles, and objects that nobody can quite identify.

Selling each item meant repeating the same manual work: take photos, figure out what it is, research a price, write a description, and post it on a marketplace. After a sale, she still had to find the physical item, arrange pickup or shipping, and remove any other listings.

I wanted to connect those tasks. Next Chapter is an orchestration layer around one item record, carrying its photos, notes, research, location, and selling history through the process.

## The experience

Capture starts at a client’s house. Choose the client once, then add one item or a batch of photos. Speak a note while looking at the objects. The app transcribes it and proposes which details belong to which items and fields, ready for review.

An item can start with just a photo. It gets a short permanent code that can go on a handwritten label, plus a location so it can be found later. AI research can suggest a name, description, price range, asking price, and suitable marketplace. A seller or client’s estimate stays separate from the researched value.

The aim is a natural next step: “This looks like an Instant Pot Duo. Here is the evidence, a suggested description, and a starting price for a quicker sale.” Uncertain details stay visible and editable.

## Decisions and design lessons

**The item record is the center.** A marketplace listing is one representation of an item. Keeping ownership, location, photos, and sale tasks together makes it possible to coordinate several channels without losing track of the object.

**Capture should not wait for certainty.** A photo and a spoken observation can be enough to begin. Identification and pricing become reviewable proposals, rather than prerequisites for saving an item.

**Preparing a listing is different from publishing it.** The browser helper transfers reviewed information; the seller remains in control of publication. Sale detection and delisting need confirmed provider responses before the app can call those tasks complete.

## Current state and next step

The private app supports capture, structured notes, inventory, research, listing preparation, and fulfillment tasks. The Etsy unpublished-draft transfer and Facebook Chrome helper are built, with seller-account and live-form validation still to complete. Etsy developer access is approved; eBay developer approval is pending.

The next step is to validate each marketplace handoff with the seller’s account, then enable and test live order syncing and cross-marketplace delisting. The full loop is the direction of the project, with each connection added and verified in stages.

The [public repository](https://github.com/ktakeuchi21/next-chapter-ds) documents the implementation, setup, and integration boundaries.
