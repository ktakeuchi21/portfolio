## Why I built it

I kept filling spare moments with doomscrolling, even though there was so much I wanted to learn. I'd tried learning apps, but the subscriptions felt too expensive for what I needed. Videos and podcasts helped, but choosing something and switching apps added friction.

I wanted to learn more about my interests and get better at building applications. So I figured, why not make the app I wanted to use? Interlude gives me a short lesson to pick up on my phone, audio for chores or a walk, and a place to read and reflect on my laptop. A clear stopping point matters as much as an easy start.

## What I can learn

The private app now has **six courses and 36 lesson texts**:

- **AI for product managers, AI for software development, and AI in pharma and healthcare:** eight lessons each.
- **AI Engineering: model decisions, evaluate and improve, and reliable systems:** four lessons each, with original diagrams and optional practice.

The newer courses explore concepts from Chip Huyen's *AI Engineering* through original teaching grounded in cited primary sources. Diagram descriptions also appear in the narration scripts. They do not reproduce the book. These lessons are available to read; their audio is pending recovery of an earlier interrupted narration request.

## Decisions and design lessons

**Make the next step obvious.** Personal use led to a home screen that resumes my current lesson, a 1.5× audio default, and a footer player that opens the lesson without losing playback.

**Teach directly.** Repeated spoken source introductions got in the way. New lessons explain the idea first and keep routine attribution in the reading view.

**Preserve work when something fails.** Interrupted audio generation needs saved-output recovery and honest cost accounting. A new lesson version should also preserve earlier notes, progress, and audio.

## Current state and next step

The running-cost target is under $10 a month. The next question is whether regular use helps me remember and apply more, not just finish lessons. I haven't measured that yet. The [public source and build notes](https://github.com/ktakeuchi21/interlude) document the implementation and experiments.
