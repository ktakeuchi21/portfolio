## The product question

How can patient-access work keep moving when the next useful action depends on incomplete information? A prescription can pass through enrollment, coverage checks, prior authorization, affordability support, and pharmacy coordination. Each handoff can lose the reason a case is waiting.

Patient Access Orchestration makes those decisions visible through a synthetic demonstration. I framed the product strategy, designed the workflow and human-work model, and directed hands-on prototyping and evaluation. The intended users are the operations teams coordinating administrative access, rather than clinicians making treatment decisions.

## The experience

A case advances to a checkpoint. The viewer supplies a condition, such as a missing document, an unanswered inquiry, or a payer request. The platform then chooses a response. The workflow shows the selected channel, supporting evidence, recorded events, and the next uncertainty.

Operations brings exceptions into a human queue, where someone reviews sources and records an accountable resolution. Measurement compares strategies across the same synthetic cases. An experience gallery lets a visitor revisit recorded scenes without creating another transaction. Together, these views connect individual decisions to their operational consequences.

## Decisions and design lessons

**A delivered transaction is not a resolved case.** Technical delivery, a payer determination, dispensing, and therapy initiation need separate evidence. Keeping these states distinct avoids a reassuring status that outruns what is actually known.

**Stopping can be the correct behavior.** Bounded retries, unresolved endings, and reviewed human handback make responsibility explicit. A workflow should preserve uncertainty when another automated attempt cannot resolve it.

**Averages hide important tradeoffs.** The synthetic comparisons show why fewer human touches are not automatically a better outcome. Earlier review can help complex cases even when progressive automation reduces work overall. Segment-level questions are more useful than declaring one strategy universally superior.

## Current state and next step

The public demo includes workflow exploration, Operations, and Measurement. Documented rule, parity, and browser checks examine the simulation’s consistency; they do not establish real patient outcomes or standards certification. Voice, extraction, and AI-style recommendations are simulated.

A useful next experiment is to shorten the automated fallback budget for complex exceptions while holding the cases and evidence rules fixed. The [full project experience](https://patient-access-orchestration.ktakeuchi.chatgpt.site/) provides the deeper product and measurement context.
